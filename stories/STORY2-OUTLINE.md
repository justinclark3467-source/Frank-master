# STORY 2: THE FAIR'S LIGHT

## World's Columbian Exposition, Chicago 1893

---

### Main Characters

**BASIL CRAIN** — 32, self-taught inventor. Brilliant but eccentric. Childlike enthusiasm, talks to his machines, dresses in rumpled suits. Creates electrical "thinking machines."

**ELEANOR VANCE** — 24, daughter of wealthy steel magnate. Educated, curious. Sneaks away from social circle to explore the fair. Enchanted by Basil's wonder.

**GRANDFATHER AUGUSTUS VANCE** — 72, industrial titan. Hasn't appeared in public in 7 years — because his wife is bedridden and he's been caring for her. He's been secretly reading Eleanor's letters to grandmother.

**GRANDMOTHER CLARA VANCE** — 70s, bedridden, fading. Eleanor writes to her. Grandpa reads the letters first, then gives them to his wife.

**MR. HARRISON VANCE** — Eleanor's father. Arranges engagement to Senator's son. Opposes Basil.

**MRS. CATHERINE VANCE** — Eleanor's mother. Quiet, defers to husband. Present but not dominant.

---

### Social Circle (Pop In and Out)

**VIVIAN PENWORTH** — 25, Eleanor's close "friend." Married to Arthur. Perfect society girl. Jealous of Eleanor's independence. Starts rumors about her connection to Basil.

**ARTHUR PENWORTH** — 28, Vivian's husband. Banker. Belongs to men's club at the fair. Notices Basil, spreads talk to Harrison.

**CONSTANCE BELMONT** — 23, shy. Secretly agrees with Eleanor but too afraid to say so. Provides gossip to Eleanor, then accidentally lets things slip.

---

### Plot Summary

**ACT 1 — THE MEETING**
Basil has a booth at the fair demonstrating his "Crain Lightning Engine." Eleanor stops, asks questions. They meet repeatedly. He's smitten but knows his place.

Social scene: Eleanor sneaks away from dinners and galas to visit the fairgrounds. She meets Basil at his booth. The Penworths notice her absence from events.

**ACT 2 — THE RISING CONFLICT**
Eleanor's father arranges engagement to Senator's son. She's trapped.
She sneaks to see Basil, admits her feelings. He pulls back: "I have nothing."
German agents working with rival industrialists sabotage Basil's workshop. Blueprints stolen. Lightning engine sabotaged.
Rumors spread: Vivian Penworth whispers at dinners. Arthur mentions Basil to Harrison. Dad confronts Eleanor — learns of her connection to the inventor.

**ACT 3 — THE TWIST**
Basil arrested — framed for stealing from German delegation. Evidence planted. Ruined.
Eleanor's father's maid (working for Germans) steals Basil's notes. Eleanor tracks her to warehouse near fairgrounds, retrieves them — but pages torn, smudged from rain.
Eleanor's engagement is fast-tracked. She's trapped tighter than before.

**ACT 4 — THE RESCUE**
Eleanor shows damaged notes to grandfather Augustus. He admits: "I've read every letter you wrote to her. She asked me to help you."
Augustus stakes his name on authenticity. He walks into hearing room — first public appearance in 7 years. Everyone freezes.
Basil fills in missing/smudged parts from memory. Only the real inventor could do that. Proven innocent.

---

### The Ending (Letters 22-24)

- Basil is freed
- Their love is explicit — she visits him
- His invention succeeds — Commission awards contract
- Epilogue: She becomes his partner. Hint of marriage, peek at future.
