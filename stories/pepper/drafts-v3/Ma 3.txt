Dearest Ma,

The rain finally broke this morning, and you could feel the difference right away. Not just in the air, but in the men as well. Everything seemed to lift a little. The ground is still heavy, and the mud is not going anywhere soon, but at least it is not falling from the sky anymore. When the sun came through, it turned the hills into something almost peaceful for a moment, if you did not look too closely at what was broken.

We have been moving more these past few days. Nothing steady, just enough to keep you from settling in one place long enough to grow comfortable. Orders come in, you pack up, you move, and then you wait again. The waiting is its own kind of work. A man starts to think too much if he lets himself, so most of us keep busy with whatever we can. Cleaning gear, checking rifles, arguing over things that do not matter just to hear something ordinary for a few minutes.

Russo spent part of yesterday explaining to a kid from Ohio why no one outside Brooklyn knows how to make a proper sandwich. The kid said at this point he would eat anything if it came warm, and that got a laugh out of everyone close enough to hear. It does not last long, but those moments matter more than you would expect.

I found myself back at the grove again. I cannot say exactly why I keep returning there, except that it feels different from everything around it. Not safer, but steadier, in a way I cannot fully explain.

This time I saw her again.

She was there, moving between the trees, careful but not afraid. I stayed where I was at first and watched. There is a way a person moves when they are unsure, and a way they move when they know exactly where they are going. She moved like she knew.

She saw me too. I could tell by the way she stopped and turned. Not startled, just aware.

I stepped forward slowly, just a few steps, keeping my hands where she could see them. I did not want her thinking I meant trouble.

She did not speak. Instead, she turned and moved deeper into the grove.

I followed.

Not at first. I stayed where I was, watching, trying to understand what she was doing. But the ground between us was open, and she was moving, and something in me wanted to know more. So I followed at a distance, careful to step where she stepped.

That was my mistake.

The moment I stepped off her path, the ground changed. Soft, unstable, the kind that shifts and pulls. I felt my foot start to sink and I stepped back quickly, finding solid ground again. When I looked up, she was gone. Not far — she could not have moved that quickly — but somewhere deeper in the grove where the shadows were thicker.

I walked the edge of where I had seen her last, looking for a path, a sign, anything. The ground everywhere looked the same to me. Uncertain. Unreliable. The opposite of how she had moved through it.

I did not call out. Some things cannot be rushed, and this was clearly one of them.

But I noticed something on my way back. The mark — the circle with the line through it — was there, carved into a stone I had not noticed before. Closer to where she had disappeared than to where I had first seen her. Like breadcrumbs, maybe. Or a test.

The fellows are holding steady. There is a rhythm to things now, not a comfortable one, but something you can move with. You wake, you move, you wait, and you do it again.

I keep thinking about the moment she looked at me. There was something in it — not fear, not welcome, but something in between. Like she was trying to decide whether I was worth the risk. I wonder what she saw. I wonder what she decided.

The mark was closer today. Not at the edge of the grove where I first saw it, but deeper in. Like she is leading me somewhere, one stone at a time. I do not know if I should follow or wait to be invited. Both feel dangerous in different ways.

Every time I go back to the grove, something changes. The marks move. The ground shifts in different places. It is like the grove itself is breathing, changing. And she is the one who knows how to read it. I am just starting to learn the language.

At night, the quiet returns, and with it the thoughts you try to keep pushed down during the day. I keep your rosary close, just like I said I would. It has not left my pocket since I came here.

Do not worry about me. I am being careful. I am not rushing into anything, and I am paying attention to what matters.

Write when you can. Tell me about the store, about Joey, about home. The small things are what keep me grounded.

Your loving son,
Frank
