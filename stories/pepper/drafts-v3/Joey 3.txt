Joey,

I followed her path this time. Not to chase her — I know better than that. But to see where she goes.

I matched my steps to hers exactly. Where she put her foot, I put mine. Slow, careful. The ground behaved differently where she walked. Firmer. More certain. Like the earth itself was holding her up.

She moved through that grove like she was reading a map inside her head. Every step certain, every turn planned. She knew exactly where she was going, and I tried to follow at a distance without letting her know.

I tested the ground where she had walked. Same spots, same places. The earth held. But when I stepped off her path — the ground shifted. Soft, uncertain. Like the grove does not want everyone to walk the same way.

The book might explain it. I keep going back to those margins, those markings. Maybe it is a record of safe ground. Maybe it marks where the soil is stable and where it is not. A farmer's guide? A surveyor's notes? I am still guessing.

She looked back at me once. Just for a moment. I cannot tell what she was thinking. Maybe she knew I was following. Maybe she was deciding whether to trust me. Maybe she was just surprised.

I am not telling anyone else about this. Not yet.

The more I study, the less I understand. But I keep going back. There is something in this place that I cannot walk away from.

I keep returning to the grove every chance I get. Something pulls me back — her, maybe, or the book, or both. I do not know what this is becoming.

How is the store? How is Ma?

Write when you can.

Sal got hit last week. Not bad, but bad enough. A graze to the shoulder. He will be fine, but it scared all of us. You never get used to seeing someone go down.

I think about home more than I should. About the store, about Ma, about whether you are safe. The war feels far away some days and too close others.

I wonder if I will ever understand what the book is for. Maybe it is nothing. Maybe it is everything. I cannot tell anymore.

Stay sharp.

Frank
