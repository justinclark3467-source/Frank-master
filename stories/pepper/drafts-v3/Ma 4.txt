Dearest Ma,

Things have begun to move faster here, not in a way that feels sudden, but in a way that does not give you time to settle your thoughts before something else is already happening. The days are fuller now, and the quiet moments do not last as long as they used to. You start to feel like you are part of something that is always in motion, whether you are ready for it or not.

We moved out before first light yesterday, and for a while it felt like the whole world was holding its breath. No wind, no sound except boots on damp ground and the occasional shift of gear where it should not be. It is strange how loud the smallest things can feel when everything else is quiet. A man coughing, a strap pulling tight, the scrape of a boot on stone. You hear everything.

Russo nearly took half the line down not twenty minutes in. He caught his foot on something he did not see and went forward hard enough that the man behind him had to grab him to keep him from making a real mess of things. Sal told him if he made that kind of noise again he would leave him there as a warning to the rest of us. That got a few quiet laughs, enough to break the tension for a moment.

We pushed through that stretch and held near a low rise that gave us just enough cover to regroup. The ground there felt different underfoot, firmer than the rest, even after all the rain. I would not have thought much of it before, but now I notice things like that. It is strange how quickly your attention changes when you start to see patterns where you did not before.

I saw her again.

This time she did not disappear the way she had before. She stayed in sight longer, moving through a section of the grove I was beginning to recognize. I watched from the edge, not sure if I should step forward or stay back.

She stopped. Not moving away, not moving closer. Just stopped. And she looked at me.

I did not move either. We stayed like that for a moment, the two of us, nothing between us but the trees and the broken ground. Then she turned and walked a few paces into a stretch of the grove I had not tried before. She stopped again and looked back.

She was showing me something.

I stepped forward carefully, testing each step. The ground held. Not the way it had looked, not soft or uncertain, but solid underfoot in a way I had not expected.

She moved again, deeper, and I followed. Not close enough to touch, not close enough to speak, but close enough to see the way she moved — careful, certain, like she was reading something written in the ground itself.

When we reached a section of broken wall at the far edge of the grove, she stopped near a stone I recognized. The mark was there. The same circle with the line through it, carved clean into the surface.

She looked at me, then at the mark, then back at me. She did not say anything. She did not need to.

I understood that she was letting me see. Not trusting me yet — not close to that — but letting me closer than before. A step, maybe. But a step nonetheless.

She turned and moved off then, the same way she always does, quiet and certain, leaving me standing there with more questions than I had before, but also with something else.

Understanding.

Not complete, not yet. But enough to know that what I saw in this place is not nothing. The marks, the way the ground changes, the way she moves — it is all connected. Something built into this land, something older than the war, older than the roads.

The fellows are holding steady. The rhythm of things has settled into something we can manage, even if it is not something we would ever choose. You wake, you move, you wait, and you do it again. Men find ways to carry it, each in their own way.

At night, the quiet still comes, though it feels different now. Not empty, but watchful. Like something is there, just beyond what you can see.

I keep your rosary close. That has not changed. I reach for it more often than I would admit, but it helps steady things when my thoughts start to move too far ahead of me.

Do not worry about me. I am being careful. I am not stepping into anything without thinking it through. I am learning, and I am paying attention to what matters.

I do not know her name yet. But I know that something is happening here, something that goes beyond the war. And I am starting to think I was meant to find it.

Write when you can. Tell me about Joey, about the store, about home. The small things are what keep me tethered.

Your loving son,
Frank
