Dearest Ma,
I need to tell you something that I have been putting off. The distraction that started as curiosity has become something else. It has become all I can think about, and it is costing us.
Two nights ago we were moving through a position that should have been simple. A short stretch between two ridges, nothing that should have required more than attention. But my mind was elsewhere, on the grove, on the marks, on whether she was somewhere in those tunnels or somewhere farther. I missed the signal. Just for a second, but that was enough. The Germans pushed a patrol through a gap that would not have been there if I had been paying attention.
No one died because of it, but Sal took a graze to the shoulder. Not bad, but enough to put him out of action for a while. The look he gave me when they pulled him back was not anger. It was worse. It was worry.
Russo finally lost his humor about it. He did not yell, which is how you know it is serious. He just looked at me and said, "You gotta come back to us, Pepper. You're not here alone."
Petey did not say anything. He never does when it matters. But he started staying closer, always within arm's reach when we moved, like he was ready to pull me out of something.
I have been thinking about what they said. About coming back. About the fact that I am not here for the war or for the glory or for any reason except that I was pulled into it, and the only thing that matters now is getting through it in one piece so I can come home.
I have not been back to the grove since that mistake. I cannot afford to be distracted again. The price is too high, and the men I serve with should not have to pay it.
But I think about her. I think about Lucia. I think about whether she is still out there, still moving through those tunnels, still waiting for something or someone. And I wonder if the reason I cannot let her go is because I finally found something in this war that does not feel like war.
I am going to try to focus. I am going to try to be the man they need me to be, the soldier they can count on, the brother Joey would be proud of. And when this is all over, I will find my way back to the grove. I will find her.
Until then, I hold the rosary and I think about home. I think about the store, and the kitchen, and you making breakfast before the sun is fully up. I think about Joey and the way he pretends to be annoyed when I mess with the register.
We are moving again tomorrow. I need to keep my head in the game. I need to be here, fully here, for the men who are counting on me.
Do not worry about me more than you have to. I am trying to be better.
Your loving son,
Frank
