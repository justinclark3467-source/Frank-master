Dearest Ma,
I am writing this in the kitchen. The kitchen. I am sitting at the table where I have sat my whole life, and I am looking at the window where the morning light comes through, and I am smelling your cooking, and I cannot believe I am here.
We made it. Lucia and I. The ship, the train, the bus, all of it. It took weeks longer than it should have because of my leg, but we made it. And now I am home.
She is here, Ma. Lucia is here. She is standing in the doorway and she is looking at the apartment like it is something from another world, and in a way it is. She has never seen anything like this. She has never been anywhere like this.
I watched her face when she walked in. The way her eyes moved over everything, the way she reached out and touched the counter, the way she looked at the window and then at me and smiled. She said, "It is small, but it is yours."
I told her it was ours.
You should have seen Joey. When we walked through that door, he just stood there. He did not move, did not speak, just stared. And then he crossed the room and grabbed me so hard I thought he would break something, and he held on and he said nothing. Nothing. That is how you know it is real.
And then he looked at Lucia, and he smiled, and he said, "So you are the one."
She laughed. That laugh I have not been able to forget since I heard it in the grove. And she said, "And you must be Joey."
They got along immediately. Of course they did. Joey has that way about him.
I have not told her everything yet. There is time for that. For now, she is learning the store, learning the neighborhood, learning what it is like to live in Brooklyn instead of the hills of Italy. She asks questions about everything, and I love answering them.
Tomorrow we are going to show her the city. Coney Island, if my leg can handle it. She has never seen the ocean.
I am happy, Ma. Truly happy. And I am here.
Your loving son,
Frank
