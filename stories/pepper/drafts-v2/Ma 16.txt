Dearest Ma,

I thought the scare with Sal would be enough to snap me out of it. I was wrong. The mind does what it wants, and mine wants her.

Two days after Sal got hit, we were in a firefight near a broken village. Everything was chaos, and in chaos you do what you have trained to do. You move, you take cover, you return fire. But I hesitated. Just for a moment, just long enough to check a shadow that looked like it might be movement, just long enough to wonder if it was her.

That hesitation nearly cost Petey his life. A German got a clean shot off because I was not where I should have been. The bullet caught his arm. He went down hard. Not dead — he is too stubborn to die easily — but bad enough that they had to evacuate him back. He will live, but I do not know when I will see him again.

Russo was furious. Not at the Germans. At me. He grabbed me by the collar and said things I will not repeat, things that needed saying. He told me I was going to get us all killed, that I was thinking with something other than my head, that whatever I was chasing was not worth losing the few men we have left.

He was right.

I have not been back to the grove since that village. I have not let myself think about it. Every time the thought starts to pull, I pull back harder. I focus on the ground in front of me, on the next step, on the next order. I try to be the soldier they need.

But the dreams are getting worse. I dream about the tunnels. I dream about her voice, the way it sounded when she first spoke to me. I dream about the marks on the stones, and the book, and what might be underneath all of this. And I dream about the war ending, about coming home, about walking through the store door and feeling normal again.

Sal is going to be okay. He lost a lot of blood but they got him back in time. He will have a scar, and he will complain about it for the rest of his life. That is just who he is.

Russo has not forgiven me yet. He is not the type to hold a grudge, but this is different. He is scared, and fear makes men angry.

I keep your rosary close. Every night, I hold it and I think about what you would say if you could see me now. I think you would tell me the same thing Joey tells me when things get hard: "Get your head right, or get out of the way."

I am trying to get my head right.

The book has become my obsession. I study it every night, every spare moment. The marks in the margins — the ones that look like herbs and recipes — they are a cipher, a code that maps the ground. I am starting to understand what they mean. Each symbol corresponds to a place on the surface, a path underground, a safe spot or a dangerous one. The book is a map to the tunnels, to the hidden network that runs beneath these hills.

I think about Lucia every time I study it. I wonder if she learned to read it the same way I am learning now. I wonder if her mother taught her, or her grandmother. I wonder how many generations have carried this knowledge, this secret.

I wonder if she is thinking about me.

I am going to try to be better, Ma. I am going to focus on what is in front of me. But I cannot stop wondering about what is underneath.

Every day, I learn something new from the book. Every day, the marks make a little more sense. And every day, I think about going back to the grove. About finding Lucia. About asking her what all of this means.

But not yet. I am not ready. I need to be sure I can do this without putting the others in danger.

There is something else I need to tell you, Ma. Something I have not said in any letter yet. I am in love with her. I know it sounds crazy — I have known her for such a short time, and most of that time I have been apart from her. But it is real. It is the strongest feeling I have ever had, stronger than the fear, stronger than the war.

I think about her all the time. I think about her dark eyes and the way she looked at me when we first met. I think about her voice, low and careful, like someone who has learned not to trust easily. I think about what it would be like to bring her home, to have her meet you and Joey, to show her the store and the kitchen and the life I had before all of this.

I think about a future with her. And that is what keeps me going.

Do not worry about me. I am being more careful now. I am watching more and thinking less.

You just keep taking care of yourself. That is what I carry with me more than anything else. That is what makes me want to come home.

Your loving son,
Frank
