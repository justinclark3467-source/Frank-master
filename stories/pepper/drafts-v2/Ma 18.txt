Dearest Ma,

I went back to the grove.

I told myself I would not, but the intercepted message changed everything. If they know about the network, if they are trying to shut it down, then she is in danger. And I could not sit here and do nothing.

I went at night, the way I always did. The path was the same, the stones were the same, but this time something was different. The marks were not just visible — they were active. Freshly cleaned, the circle and line standing out against the old stone. Someone was there. Recently. Someone had been tending to the markers.

I followed the marks, deeper than I had ever gone. The tunnel entrance was hidden behind a section of wall that looked like it had not moved in centuries. But it moved for me. Behind it, stairs led down into darkness.

I could hear things. Voices, faint and far away. The underground was not just a passage. It was alive. There were families down there — Italians, Germans, people who had run from the war and found their way here.

And then I saw her.

Lucia.

She was there, in a chamber that opened up from the tunnel, surrounded by others. She looked at me and I looked at her and neither of us spoke for a long moment. Then she smiled, just slightly, and said, "I knew you'd come back."

She told me everything. The network, the tunnels, the resistance that has been here for generations. This is not just a hiding place. It is an escape route. A way for people to get to safety.

Her family has been running it for years. The book — my book — it is their ledger. A codebook. Those marks in the margins, the ones that look like herbs and recipes, are a cipher. A way of marking the safe paths, the dangerous ground. The book was given to her mother, and her mother before her. And now it is in my hands.

But they found a section of the tunnel. They are getting closer. She needs help, and she asked for mine.

I said yes.

I do not know what happens next. But I know that this is right. This is why I am here.

I am going to help her. And when it is over, I will come home to you.

I found the book made sense now. All those weeks studying the marks, the symbols, the cipher in the margins — it was all preparation for this. The book was a map to her world. It was a key to the tunnels. And now I am standing inside them, with the woman I love, ready to fight beside her.

The Germans are closing in on a section that will expose everyone if they find it. We have to move them. All of them. To a safer section of tunnels that leads to the next waypoint. It is dangerous, and it has to happen soon.

But with my help, with the men I have beside me, we can do it. Russo and Sal are ready. They believe in what we are doing.

Do not worry about me, Ma. I am doing what is right. And finally, I am doing something that feels like it matters.

For the first time since I arrived in this country, I am not just surviving. I am fighting for something. For Lucia. For the families in those tunnels. For a future.

When she smiled at me in that chamber, Ma, I knew everything was going to be okay. I knew why I came to this country. I knew why I found the book. I knew why I kept going back to the grove. It was all leading to this.

She told me about the book. About how it has been in her family for generations. About how her grandmother used to sit and write in the margins, turning recipes into codes, herbs into markers. It was a way to pass the knowledge down without anyone knowing. A secret language hidden in plain sight.

And now I am part of it. I am part of the resistance. I am part of something bigger than the war.

I think about you, Ma. I think about what you would say if you could see me now. I think you would be proud. I think you would tell me to be careful, but also that you understand why I am doing this.

Joey would understand too. He has always been the one who knows how to handle things. I wish he was here. But I have to do this on my own.

I got another letter from Joey. He did not say much, but I could tell something was wrong at home. Something big. The Greek — I could feel it in his words. I wish I could be there. But I have to finish this first.

I will come home to you. I promise.

Your loving son,
Frank
