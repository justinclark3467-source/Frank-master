Dearest Ma,

Three weeks now without writing. I have been trying to find my way back to being the man I was before all of this, the one who could focus, who could do the job without distraction. It is harder than I thought. The mind does not just flip a switch.

But I have been trying. Every day I wake up and I make a decision to be here, fully here. And every day that decision gets a little easier.

We were moved to a new position last week. Higher ground, better vantage. The kind of place where you can see everything and nothing at the same time. From here, you can see the ridge where the grove is. Not clearly, just a shape in the distance, but enough to know it is there.

I have not gone back. I made that promise to myself, and I am keeping it.

But something happened today that changes everything.

We intercepted a message. Not our boys — theirs. Germans talking about a network. A hidden network underneath the hills. They used words like "Kammer" and "Bewegung," the kind of words that do not mean much unless you know what you are looking for. But I know. I have been living at the edge of it since I arrived.

They know about the tunnels. And they are trying to shut them down.

That means Lucia is still out there. Still working. Still part of whatever this is. And she is in more danger than I knew.

I have a decision to make. I can stay here, stay focused, be the soldier they need. Or I can go back to the grove, find her, and figure out what is really happening. The first option keeps me alive. The second might help end this faster.

I am going to think about it. I am going to make the right choice.

The book is becoming clearer to me. The marks in the margins — the herbs and recipes — they are a cipher. A code that maps the underground. Each symbol is a location, a path, a safe place. I am beginning to understand what the previous owner was trying to preserve.

I think about Lucia every time I open it. I wonder if she knows this book. I wonder if she was taught to read it.

Russo talked to me today. Actually talked, not yelled. He said he can see me trying to come back, and he appreciates it. He said we need each other, now more than ever. He is right.

I got a letter from Joey. He did not say much about the store. But I could tell he was dealing with something alone. Something big. I wish I could be there.

But I cannot think about that now. I have to finish this first.

The intercepted message plays over and over in my mind. The Germans know about the tunnels. They are hunting the network. And if they find Lucia — if they find the families hiding in those passages — they will not hesitate. They will kill everyone.

I cannot let that happen. I cannot let her die in those tunnels, alone and afraid, with no one to help her.

I am going to make a decision soon. And when I do, I will write and tell you everything.

But for now, I am going to think about it. I am going to study the book more. I am going to understand what I am getting into before I jump.

The Germans are getting closer. I can feel it. And so is my answer.

Every night I hold your rosary and I think about what happens if I go back. If I find Lucia. If I help her protect the network. We could save hundreds of people. Families. Children. People who have been hiding in the dark for months, waiting for the war to end.

Or I could die down there. I could be killed by a German patrol, or by a collapsed tunnel, or by my own distraction.

Either way, I am going to have to make a choice. And soon.

I keep thinking about the book. About how it maps the tunnels. About how it was passed down through generations. About Lucia, learning to read it as a child, her mother teaching her the symbols. I wonder if she ever imagined someone else would hold it. I wonder if she ever imagined finding someone like me.

I wonder if she is thinking about me too.

Whatever happens, I want you to know that I found something important here, Ma. Something worth fighting for. Not just the war. Something deeper. Something that has been here longer than any of us.

I will write again soon. And when I do, I will have an answer.

Stay safe, Ma. Keep the light on for me.

Your loving son,
Frank

Do not worry about me. I am trying to make the right calls.

You just keep taking care of yourself. That is what I carry with me more than anything else.
