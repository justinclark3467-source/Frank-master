Dearest Ma,

I am sitting in the garden outside our house. The sun is warm, the air smells like the hills, and our son is playing in the grass. His name is Antonio, after Lucia's father. He is three years old now, and he has her eyes.

Lucia is inside, cooking. The smell drifts out the window, and it makes me think of you, of the kitchen in Brooklyn, of mornings when everything was simple. I wish you could smell this. I wish you could see this.

We built this house together. Lucia and I. It took two years, but we did it. A small place on the hillside, with a garden and a view that stretches out forever. We have chickens, and a goat, and more cats than we can count. Antonio loves the cats. He chases them around the garden and laughs when they run.

The town is coming back. Slowly, but surely. The people who lived in the tunnels, the ones we helped escape, they are all here now. We rebuild together. It is hard work, but it is good work. The kind of work that makes you tired at the end of the day and grateful for every morning that follows.

Lucia's mother visits us every week. She brings bread and stories, and she plays with Antonio while we work. We call her Nonna, and Antonio adores her. She teaches him Italian songs, and he sings them back to her, and it is the most beautiful thing I have ever heard.

Joey writes. Every month, a letter arrives, full of news from the store, from the apartment, from Brooklyn. He is doing well. The store is thriving, better than ever. He says he is proud of me, and I do not know how to respond to that. I am proud of him.

Ma, I wish you could see this. I wish you could meet Antonio. I wish you could sit in this garden and feel the sun and know that everything worked out. The war, the tunnels, the mission, the injury, all of it. It was worth it. All of it was worth it.

The book is on the shelf here. The ledger. I have not opened it in months, but I know it is there. I know what it means to me. I know what it means to Lucia. It is a reminder of where we came from, and what we went through, and how we found each other.

Lucia calls for dinner. Antonio is running to the table. I have to go.

I love you, Ma. I will see you again someday.

Sometimes I think about Brooklyn. About the store, about the apartment, about the kitchen with the morning light. I think about Joey arguing with customers, and about you making breakfast before the sun is fully up. I think about the life I left behind, and the life I have built here.

They are different lives, but they are both mine. And both of them led me to this moment. Both of them led me to Lucia, to Antonio, to this garden on the hillside.

I am grateful. For everything. For the war that brought me to Italy. For the book that led me to Lucia. For the tunnel network that gave me a purpose. For the injury that brought me home. For all of it.

I am the luckiest man in the world.

Come visit us someday, Ma. Bring Joey. Bring the girl at the store that he likes. Show Antonio the ocean. Let him run through the streets the way we did.

I wish you could see this. I wish you could meet everyone. But I know you will, someday.

I love you, Ma.

Every morning, I wake up and I look at the sun coming through the window, and I think about you. About the kitchen in Brooklyn. About the store. About everything that made me who I am.

I am who I am because of you. Because of Joey. Because of the store. Because of the life we built together.

And now I have a new life. A new family. A new home.

But you will always be my home, Ma. You will always be the heart of everything I am.

I think about the book often. The ledger. I brought it with me from Brooklyn, and now it sits on the shelf here, next to the books in Italian that Lucia's mother gave us. Sometimes Antonio pulls it down and looks at the pictures, not understanding what they mean. I smile when I watch him. One day I will tell him the story. One day I will explain what the book meant, what it did, how it helped me find his mother.

But not yet. For now, he is just a boy playing in the garden. For now, he is just a boy who loves the cats and the chickens and the goat. For now, that is enough.

Everything is enough.

I love you, Ma. I will see you again soon.

Your loving son,
Frank
